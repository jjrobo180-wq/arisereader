An Accelerated Reader alternative that's free for families.

A.R.I.S.E. Reader gives students comprehension quizzes for the books they read, rewards that keep them reading, and gives teachers a clear view of every student's progress. It's free for students and families, and teachers pay $10 a month per 30 students.

Try it with your class →See A.R.I.S.E. Reader

Free for every family

Students and parents with up to three kids never pay. Reading at home doesn't depend on a school license, and teachers pay one simple price per 30 students.

Quizzes for the books kids actually read

When a book isn't in the library yet, a student can ask for a quiz. It's checked against real books and approved first, then the student is told it's ready.

Rewards that keep them going

Every finished quiz earns points toward levels, streaks, a leaderboard rank and printable certificates, plus Reader Coins to spend in the game worlds.

Built for every learner

Read-aloud and eye-gaze-friendly quiz modes are included, and Eye Gazer gives non-verbal learners a calm screen of big choices to read alongside their class.

Questions teachers and parents ask

Is A.R.I.S.E. Reader free?

Yes for students and families: A.R.I.S.E. Reader is free for every student and for parents with up to 3 linked children. Teachers pay $10 a month per 30 students, which also includes A.R.I.S.E. Math, History and Connect.

Can students take a quiz on a book that isn't in the library?

They can ask for one. A student requests a quiz for the book they read; the request is checked against real books and approved before the quiz goes live, and the student is told when it's ready.

Can a student take the same book quiz twice to raise their score?

No. Each student can take a book's quiz once, so points reflect a first, honest attempt.

Do teachers see each student's progress?

Yes. Teachers see quiz attempts, scores and points for every student, and can run live quiz games, build their own quizzes and control game time.

Does it work at home as well as at school?

Yes. It runs in the browser on computers, tablets and phones, and parents can link their children's accounts to follow along.

Does it support students with disabilities?

Yes. There are read-aloud and eye-gaze-friendly quiz modes, and A.R.I.S.E. Eye Gazer gives non-verbal and eye-gaze learners a calmer home screen with big choices.

Is A.R.I.S.E. connected to Accelerated Reader?

No. A.R.I.S.E. Reader is an independent program built by a teacher. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Renaissance Learning.
